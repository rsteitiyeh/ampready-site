Journeyman electrician flashcards work best on facts you have to recall instantly and can't reason your way into: NEC table values, wire ampacities, conduit and box fill numbers, and clearance rules. They're the wrong tool for load calculation questions, which need worked practice, not recall.

Every state journeyman exam mixes two question types: pure recall (what does Table 310.16 list as the ampacity of 10 AWG copper THHN?) and applied calculation (size the service for this dwelling). Flashcards close the recall gap fast. Calculation problems need a different study method: worked problems, not cards.

## What should go on a journeyman electrician flashcard?

Put fixed reference numbers on cards, not concepts you already understand from fieldwork. Good candidates: [NEC](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) table values (conductor ampacity, box fill per conductor size, conduit fill percentages), minimum clearances (working space under 110.26, service clearances under 230.9), and disconnect rules (up to six disconnecting means allowed at one service under 230.71).

Skip anything you can already derive on the spot. If you understand how to apply Ohm's Law, a card that just says "V = IR" wastes a slot. Save cards for numbers you'd otherwise have to look up mid-exam.

## How many flashcards do you actually need?

Most journeyman candidates build somewhere between 150 and 300 cards, organized by NEC chapter. That covers wiring methods (Chapter 3), calculations (Chapter 2), and equipment (Chapters 4 through 6) without turning the deck into a second code book. A deck past 400 cards usually means you're copying whole sections instead of extracting the one number you'll actually be tested on.

## Physical or digital flashcards for the journeyman exam?

Digital decks (Anki, Quizlet) win on scheduling: spaced-repetition software resurfaces cards you miss more often than ones you've nailed several times running, so study time concentrates on weak spots instead of re-reviewing what you already know. Physical cards win on exam-day mimicry: most state exams, including Texas TDLR's two-part journeyman exam (59 general items and 26 Texas-specific code items, per the [TDLR candidate handbook](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/individuals/journeyman-electrician.htm)), are proctored and closed-device, so flipping a paper card is closer to the real recall you'll do in the exam room than tapping a phone.

A workable split: build the deck digitally for the scheduling engine, then print or handwrite the 30 to 40 cards you keep missing for a final closed-book pass the week of the exam.

## What's the best flashcard study schedule before exam day?

| Timeframe | What to do with the deck |
|---|---|
| 6 to 8 weeks out | Build the full deck by NEC chapter as you study |
| 3 to 5 weeks out | Daily review of the full deck with spaced repetition |
| 1 to 2 weeks out | Isolate the cards you keep missing; drop the rest |
| Final 2 to 3 days | Physical cards only, missed-card pile, no new material |

## Do flashcards work for NEC calculation questions?

Not directly. Flashcards handle the inputs to a calculation (a 3 VA per square foot general lighting load under 220.41, or 1,500 VA per small-appliance circuit under 220.52) but they don't build the sequencing skill of stringing those inputs into a full service calculation. That takes timed, worked practice problems, since most state exams run on a hard clock, California's DIR General Electrician exam among them at 100 questions in four hours with a 70 percent passing score, per the [California Department of Industrial Relations](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/ecu/1a.html).

Use cards to memorize table values fast, then spend the time saved running full load calculations by hand.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between a flashcard deck and a study guide for the journeyman exam?**
A study guide teaches the concept with worked examples and explanations. A flashcard deck drills the specific numbers and rules once you already understand the concept. Most candidates work through a study guide first, then pull flashcards from it for the facts they keep forgetting.

**Are free journeyman electrician flashcard decks any good?**
Free decks on Quizlet and Anki's shared library range from solid to outdated. Check the NEC edition cited in the deck description before trusting it. A deck built against an older code cycle will teach the wrong service-disconnect and AFCI rules for a current-cycle exam.

**How long before the exam should I start using flashcards?**
Start building the deck as you study each NEC chapter, roughly six to eight weeks out, so review time and content building don't compete for the same hours. Cramming a flashcard deck in the final week only works for facts you've already seen a few times.

**Can flashcards replace a full exam prep course?**
No. Flashcards handle recall; prep courses and study guides build the calculation skills and code-navigation speed that make up most of a journeyman exam. Treat flashcards as one piece of a study plan, not the whole plan.
