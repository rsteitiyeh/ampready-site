Journeyman electrician license requirements are not nationally uniform: some states issue a statewide journey-level credential, while others leave individual licensing to cities or regulate only electrical contractors. The [Texas regulator](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/individuals/journeyman-electrician.htm) documents an 8,000-hour route and a [70 percent exam cut score](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/pdf/Candidate-Information-Bulletin.pdf), but those figures are not universal. The tables below quote only figures published by regulators or official exam administrators.

## Which states license journeyman electricians statewide?

Statewide individual licensing is common across the West, Upper Midwest, and Northeast, but it is not the rule everywhere. A worker in a local-license state must check the city or county where the work will occur; a state electrical-contractor license does not automatically create a personal journeyman credential.

This classification routes readers to the right licensing level; legal permission comes from the responsible board. The U.S. Department of Labor-sponsored [CareerOneStop License Finder](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/toolkit/Training/find-licenses-help.aspx) says state agencies submit occupational-license records annually, but it also warns that not every state supplies every record. State board and local code-office pages remain the controlling sources.

California illustrates the naming issue. The state calls its individual credential "general electrician certification" and requires 8,000 hours for that classification, according to the [California Department of Industrial Relations](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/ecu/EleCat.html). It is the closest statewide comparison to a journeyman credential, even though the title differs.

The most common documented pattern in this sample is several years of supervised experience followed by a code-based examination. Fees are less comparable because one regulator may quote an exam charge while another quotes an application or initial-license charge.

"Vendor schedule" is intentional. A number was omitted when the board sends candidates to an outside test provider without putting a current amount on the regulator page. That avoids copying an old candidate bulletin into a 2026 table.

The Texas row also shows why application timing and licensure timing must be separated. [TDLR allows](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/individuals/journeyman-electrician.htm) an applicant to seek exam approval at 7,000 hours, but it still requires the full 8,000 hours before issuing the license. The [PSI bulletin](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/pdf/Candidate-Information-Bulletin.pdf) lists 80 scored questions, 240 minutes, a 70 percent cut score, and a $78 examination charge.

## Are most journeyman exams based on the same threshold?

No, although 70 percent appears frequently. Alaska, California, New Hampshire, Texas, and Washington publish a 70 percent threshold in the linked sources. [Michigan requires 75 percent](https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/bcc/sections/licensing-section/exam-lic/electrical-examination-licensing-registration-application-information).

That small difference can be obscured by exam structure. [Washington requires 70 percent](https://apps.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=296-46B-960) on each examination section. [Texas's current bulletin](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/pdf/Candidate-Information-Bulletin.pdf) describes one scored journeyman exam with 80 scored items. [New Hampshire's rules](https://gc.nh.gov/rules/state_agencies/elec100-400.html) describe 110 questions, divided into 50 National Electrical Code questions, 50 practical-installation questions, and 10 state-law and rule questions. Those are different testing designs even where the percentage matches.

Passing scores also do not transfer automatically. Colorado's reciprocity page requires an active license earned through a state-administered examination and held for at least 1 year. It lists 14 partner states: Alaska, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Utah, and Wyoming. The official list is on the [Colorado Electrical Board application page](https://dpo.colorado.gov/Electrical/Applications).

## Why do fees vary so much across state tables?

Boards label different pieces of the transaction. [California's $100](https://dir.ca.gov/dlse/ECU/ECU_TestInfo.htm) is an examination fee. [Montana's $240](https://boards.bsd.dli.mt.gov/_docs/ele/journey-ele-app-24.pdf) is an application fee. [Maine publishes](https://www1.maine.gov/pfr/professionallicensing/professions/electricians/licensing/journeyman-electrician-senior-journeyman-electrician) a $150 license fee and a separate $21 background-report charge. Texas separates a [$30 state application](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/individuals/journeyman-electrician.htm) from a [$78 vendor exam](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/pdf/Candidate-Information-Bulletin.pdf).

[Nebraska's 2026 form](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/2026%20updated%20Application%20%28002%294.1.2026_4.pdf) is another example. It lists a $90 journeyman application charge and then a license fee that depends on the year of issuance within the license cycle. Quoting only one of those numbers as "the Nebraska cost" would understate what a new applicant may pay.

For budgeting, ask the board or vendor about five distinct items: application review, examination, initial credential, background check, and local registration. Add only the charges that apply to the same applicant path.

## What should an applicant verify before applying?

Start with the licensing level in the first table, then open the regulator's current application and exam bulletin. Verify which work hours count, who must sign the experience record, which code edition is tested, and whether separate law or practical sections apply.

Workers crossing state lines should check reciprocity before scheduling a second exam. Reciprocal recognition usually depends on how the original license was earned, its current standing, and how long it has been held. A license granted without examination may fail a reciprocity rule that requires a tested credential.

Finally, check local registration even after receiving a state license. In states with local licensing, the city is the primary gatekeeper. In states with statewide credentials, a municipality may still control permits and contractor registration. The person-level journey license and the business-level contractor license answer different legal questions.
