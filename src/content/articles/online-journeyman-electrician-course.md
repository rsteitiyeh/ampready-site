The main online journeyman electrician course options fall into four categories: textbook-and-video exam-prep programs (Mike Holt Enterprises), subscription video-lesson platforms (Electrician U), union-sponsored apprenticeship curricula (IBEW/NECA's NJATC-descended programs, delivered through local JATCs), and bare practice-question banks sold directly by testing vendors like PSI. They solve different problems, and picking the wrong category for what you actually need is the most common waste of course money.

None of these are interchangeable. A practice-question bank won't teach you the underlying code logic if you don't already have it from your apprenticeship. A full video course is overkill if you already know the material and just need timed reps before test day.

## The four categories, compared

| Category | Example provider | Best for | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Textbook + video exam prep | Mike Holt Enterprises | Structured, code-cycle-specific exam review | Print workbook, video modules, simulated exams |
| Subscription video lessons | Electrician U | Ongoing skill-building beyond just exam prep | Streaming video library, membership model |
| Union/JATC apprenticeship curriculum | Local IBEW/NECA JATC programs | Apprentices already enrolled in a formal program | Blended in-person and online, tied to on-the-job hours |
| Practice-question banks | PSI's own practice test products | Final timed reps close to your test date | Question sets only, no instruction |

## Textbook and video exam prep: Mike Holt Enterprises

Mike Holt Enterprises sells journeyman and master exam prep bundles tied to a specific NEC edition (their materials are typically refreshed each code cycle, so a "2023 NEC" version and a newer edition version can both be on the market at once). These bundles combine a workbook, video instruction, and simulated exams, and they're built around actually teaching the code, not just testing you on it. That makes them a stronger fit if you feel shaky on the underlying material, not just rusty on speed. Confirm you're buying the edition that matches your state's currently adopted NEC before ordering, since exam-prep publishers often sell multiple editions simultaneously.

## Subscription video platforms: Electrician U

Electrician U runs on a membership model with a large streaming library covering both exam-adjacent code topics and broader trade skills (troubleshooting, code changes, tool reviews). It's a better fit if you want ongoing access to trade content beyond a single exam cycle, less ideal if you specifically need a structured week-by-week exam countdown plan. Check current membership pricing directly, since subscription platforms change tiers and pricing more often than a one-time textbook purchase.

## Union and JATC apprenticeship programs

If you're in a union apprenticeship, your local IBEW/NECA Joint Apprenticeship and Training Committee (JATC) almost certainly already provides structured curriculum, both in-person and online, tied to your required on-the-job hours. Apprentices typically log around 2,000 hours of paid on-the-job training per year across a 4- to 5-year apprenticeship, with related technical instruction covering code, theory, and safety built into that same program ([U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook: Electricians](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/electricians.htm)). If you're already enrolled, check what your JATC provides before paying for a duplicate outside course. Non-union apprentices generally don't have this built in and are the group most likely to need an outside course.

## Bare practice-question banks

Testing vendors themselves, including PSI, sell standalone practice-question products separate from any instruction. These are useful for exactly one purpose: timed reps close to your actual test date, once you already know the material. They're the wrong tool if you're trying to learn a topic from scratch, since a bank of questions with answer keys doesn't explain the reasoning the way a course does.

## What actually matters when you're choosing

Match the course type to the gap you actually have, not to whichever one has the best marketing. If you can already work through a load calculation but you're slow, you need timed practice, not another video lecture. If you genuinely don't understand how conductor derating works, a practice bank without instruction will just frustrate you with wrong answers and no explanation.

Check that whatever you buy is current for your state's adopted NEC edition. TDLR, for example, currently references the 2023 NEC for the Texas exam even though NFPA has already published the 2026 NEC ([NFPA 70, National Electrical Code](https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-70-national-electrical-code-nec/p0070code)); a course built around the newest code edition can actively work against you if your state hasn't adopted it yet.

## A realistic combination

Most people do best combining two categories rather than betting everything on one: a structured course or your JATC curriculum for the underlying material, plus a focused practice-question tool for timed reps in the final two to three weeks before your test date. ## FAQ

**Is an online journeyman electrician course enough to pass without an apprenticeship?**
No. Every state requires documented apprenticeship or equivalent work experience hours before you're even eligible to sit for the journeyman exam. An online course supplements that experience for exam prep; it doesn't substitute for it on your license application.

**Do I need to buy a new course every time the NEC updates?**
Only if your state has adopted the new edition for testing. States lag behind NFPA's three-year code cycle by varying amounts, sometimes years, so check your state's currently adopted edition before assuming you need the newest materials.

**Are free YouTube videos enough to prepare?**
They can help with specific concepts, but they're inconsistent in quality and rarely map directly to your state's specific content outline or question format. Treat free video as a supplement to a structured course or your apprenticeship curriculum, not a replacement for either.

**What's the cheapest way to prepare if I'm already confident in the material?**
A focused practice-question tool close to your test date, rather than a full course. If you're confident in the underlying code knowledge and just need speed and timed reps, you're paying for content you don't need in a full course bundle.

**Does my union apprenticeship program already include exam prep?**
Often, yes. Most JATC-run programs build code and theory instruction into the required curriculum. Check with your local training coordinator before buying an outside course that might duplicate what you're already getting.
