Most states require 8,000 hours of supervised on-the-job experience before a journeyman electrician exam, but who issues the license, how many classroom hours are required, and what the exam looks like varies considerably by state. Some states run a single statewide licensing board; others, including Florida, Illinois, and most of New York, license journeymen at the county or city level instead.

That structural difference matters more than most candidates expect. In a statewide-licensing state, one exam and one license work anywhere in the state. In a local-licensing state, moving to a different city or county inside the same state can mean requalifying under different local rules.

## How do requirements compare across states?

| State | Licensing authority | Experience required | Exam format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | Statewide (TDLR) | 8,000 hours | Two-part exam, 59 general and 26 Texas-specific code items, per the [TDLR journeyman application page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/individuals/journeyman-electrician.htm) |
| California | Statewide (DIR, General Electrician certification) | 8,000 hours OJT plus 720 classroom hours | 100 questions, 4 hours, 70 percent passing, open-book with NEC provided, per the [California DIR](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/ecu/1a.html) |
| Washington | Statewide (L&I) | 8,000 hours plus 96 classroom hours; a registered apprenticeship becomes mandatory for new journey-level candidates starting July 2026 | NEC-based written exam |
| Florida | No statewide journeyman license; county or city boards issue locally | Typically 8,000 hours (4 years), requirements vary by local jurisdiction | Local written exam; state DBPR only licenses electrical contractors statewide, via a two-part Pearson VUE exam |
| Illinois | No statewide journeyman license; municipal boards issue locally | Roughly 8,000 hours OJT plus 720 classroom hours, per most municipal boards | Local written exam, format varies by municipality |
| New York | No statewide journeyman license; NYC issues Master/Special Electrician only, no journeyman tier | Upstate localities that do issue journeyman licenses commonly require around 8,250 hours OJT | Local written exam; NYC's Master Electrician path requires roughly 7.5 years (10,500 hours) plus written and practical exams |

This is a representative sample, not a full 50-state list. Requirements change, and local jurisdictions inside the same state can differ from each other. Always confirm current numbers with the licensing authority before applying.

## Why do some states license journeymen locally instead of statewide?

Historically, electrical licensing in the US grew out of city building departments long before most states created statewide contractor boards, and several large states, Florida, Illinois, and New York among them, never fully centralized journeyman licensing afterward. The practical effect: a journeyman electrician moving from, say, Chicago to a different Illinois municipality may need to requalify under that municipality's specific local exam and fee structure, even without leaving the state.

## What does license reciprocity look like between states?

Reciprocity is limited and inconsistent. Most states that do offer it require the applicant's original license to have been issued under substantially similar hour and exam requirements, and many require at least a written exam covering that state's specific code amendments even when hours transfer. Reciprocity agreements are negotiated state-by-state and change over time, so confirm current status directly with the destination state's board rather than relying on a general reciprocity claim.

## How often do journeyman licenses need renewal?

Renewal periods vary by state. Texas requires annual renewal at a $30 fee, along with 4 hours of continuing education each cycle, per the [TDLR renewal page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/renew/individuals/journeyman-electrician.htm). Other states run on 2 or 3-year renewal cycles instead of annual. Continuing education requirements, where they exist, are typically tied to code-cycle updates so licensees stay current on the latest adopted NEC edition.

## FAQ

**Do all states require 8,000 hours to become a journeyman electrician?**
Most do, since 8,000 hours (roughly four years of full-time work) is the informal national standard tied to typical apprenticeship length. Exact figures vary; some states set slightly higher thresholds, and a few count partial credit for trade-school classroom hours toward the total.

**Can I use my journeyman license from one state to work in another?**
Sometimes, through reciprocity agreements, but not automatically. Even where reciprocity exists, many states still require passing a shorter exam covering that state's specific code amendments. Contact the destination state's licensing board directly before assuming your existing license transfers.

**Why doesn't Florida have a statewide journeyman license?**
Florida's electrical licensing structure splits authority between the state, which licenses electrical contractors through the DBPR's Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board, and individual counties and cities, which issue journeyman-level cards locally. This split predates most modern statewide licensing systems and hasn't been consolidated.

**Does classroom instruction time count toward the experience hour requirement?**
In some states, yes, up to a limit. California's DIR certification, for example, requires 720 classroom hours as a separate component alongside the 8,000 on-the-job hours rather than substituting for them. Check your specific state's rules, since treatment of classroom credit varies.
