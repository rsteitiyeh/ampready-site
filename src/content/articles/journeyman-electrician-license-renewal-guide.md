Journeyman electrician license renewal rules differ on three things: how often you renew, what it costs, and whether you owe continuing education hours. Texas renews annually and requires 4 hours of CE. West Virginia also renews annually but requires no CE at all. Maryland and Virginia run 2 and 3-year cycles with modest CE loads, and California requires the heaviest CE burden of any state checked: 32 hours per 3-year cycle.

There's no single national rule here, and mixing up the requirements between your state and a forum post about a different state is the most common way license holders lapse. Check your own board's current page before you assume anything below still applies to your renewal.

## Does Texas require CE hours to renew a journeyman license, or just a fee?

Both. Texas requires 4 hours of continuing education per one-year renewal cycle, not a fee-only renewal.

That CE has to cover specific topics: the NEC, Texas Electrician Law under Occupations Code Chapter 1305, the Texas Administrative Rules in 16 TAC Chapter 73, and NFPA 70E safety content, completed through a TDLR-registered provider who reports your hours electronically, according to [TDLR's continuing education page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/elecce.htm). TDLR's own renewal fee schedule lists the current individual license renewal amount, but published figures vary by source and the fee schedule is revised periodically, so confirm the exact current dollar amount directly on [TDLR's fee page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/elecfees.htm) before you pay rather than relying on a third-party estimate.

## How does renewal work in states without a CE requirement?

Some states renew on a fee-only basis with no continuing education at all, and West Virginia is the clearest example.

West Virginia's journeyman and master electrician licenses expire every June 30 and can be renewed up to 3 years at a time, for $50 per year, with no continuing education requirement in the current licensing rule, per [103CSR5](https://firemarshal.wv.gov/media/39366/download), the state's Electrician Licensing Rules effective May 1, 2024. Don't confuse this with West Virginia's separate CE rule for certified Electrical Inspectors, a different license category that does carry a 14-hour annual CE requirement. If your license lapses in West Virginia, it can still be renewed without retesting within 5 years, but at double the standard fee.

## What does a multi-state renewal comparison actually look like?

Cycle length, fee, and CE hours vary independently of each other: a short cycle doesn't mean a heavy CE load, and vice versa.

| State | Renewal cycle | Fee | CE hours required | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | 1 year | Varies; confirm current amount on TDLR's fee page | 4 hours per cycle | [TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/elecce.htm) |
| West Virginia | 1 year (renewable up to 3 at once) | $50 per year | None | [WV Fire Marshal, 103CSR5](https://firemarshal.wv.gov/media/39366/download) |
| Maryland | 2 years | $18.50 (journeyperson) | 5 hours per cycle | [MD Labor, fee schedule](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/elec/elecaff.shtml) |
| Virginia | 3 years | $160 | 3 hours, focused on NEC updates | [DPOR, 18VAC50-30](https://www.dpor.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/boards/Tradesman/A501-27TRADEREGS.pdf) |
| California | 3 years | $100 | 32 hours per cycle, plus 2,000 hours of verified work experience | [DIR, Certified Electrician FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/ecu/ECU_FAQ.htm) |

California's 32-hour requirement is the outlier here, more than six times Virginia's 3-hour minimum for a cycle of the same length. If your CE requirement lapses in California, the 32-hour requirement is waived on relicensing, but you have to retake the exam for $200, which is a harder reset than most states impose for a missed CE cycle.

## What about Washington DC and other states with unclear published rules?

DC's public licensing pages confirm the renewal cycle but don't publish a clear, current fee or CE table, and that gap is itself worth knowing before you plan around it.

Washington DC's Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection confirms all trade licenses run a 2-year cycle expiring November 30 of odd-numbered years, per [DLCP's applicant guide](https://dlcp.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/DLCP/publication/attachments/Electrician.pdf), but the codified fee and CE rules in DCMR Title 17 predate DC's 2022 occupational licensing reorganization and haven't been refreshed to match. Secondary sources cite DC fees anywhere from $110 to $300 and CE anywhere from none to 18 hours for a Master Electrician, which is too wide a spread to publish as fact. If you're renewing in DC, call DLCP directly to confirm the current fee and CE requirement rather than trusting a cached number, ours included.

## How do I avoid missing a renewal deadline?

Track your specific state's cycle length and any grace period, and complete CE inside the cycle it's meant for, not after the fact.

Most boards don't accept CE completed after your license has expired as credit toward that cycle. Virginia gives a 30-day grace period to renew without a reinstatement fee, and up to 24 months post-expiration before you'd need a new application and exam, according to [DPOR's tradesman regulations](https://www.dpor.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/boards/Tradesman/A501-27TRADEREGS.pdf). Georgia, which licenses electrical contractors rather than individual journeymen, now requires all CE to be logged through the CE Broker platform starting January 1, 2026, per the [Georgia Secretary of State's electrical contractor page](https://sos.ga.gov/page/electrical-contractor-continuing-education), a reminder that reporting mechanics change even when the hour requirement doesn't.

Keep a copy of every CE completion certificate for at least a year past your renewal date. Maryland's board, for example, requires CE completed within the 24 months preceding renewal, so a certificate that's timestamped wrong relative to your cycle can get rejected even if the hours themselves are legitimate.

## FAQ

**Does TDLR require continuing education to renew a journeyman electrician license?**
Yes. Texas requires 4 hours of CE per one-year renewal cycle, covering the NEC, Texas Electrician Law, state administrative rules, and NFPA 70E safety content, completed through a TDLR-registered provider.

**Does West Virginia require CE hours for journeyman electrician license renewal?**
No. West Virginia's current licensing rule requires only the $50 annual fee, with no continuing education for journeyman or master electrician renewal. A separate CE rule applies only to certified Electrical Inspectors.

**How much does it cost to renew a journeyman electrician license in Maryland?**
Maryland's journeyperson renewal fee is $18.50 for a 2-year cycle, plus 5 hours of continuing education related to the NEC, NFPA-adopted codes, or Maryland's licensing statute.

**What is the journeyman electrician license renewal process in DC?**
DC renews trade licenses on a 2-year cycle expiring November 30 of odd-numbered years, but current fee and CE figures aren't clearly published on the board's site. Call DLCP directly to confirm before you plan your renewal.

**Which state has the highest CE requirement for electrician license renewal?**
Of the states checked here, California has the heaviest load: 32 hours of continuing education per 3-year cycle, plus 2,000 hours of verified work experience, far above Virginia's 3-hour minimum for the same cycle length.
