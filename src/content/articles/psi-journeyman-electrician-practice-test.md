PSI is the testing vendor, not the rule-maker. Dozens of state licensing boards contract with PSI Services LLC to deliver their journeyman electrician exam by computer, but each state board sets its own question count, time limit, passing score, and reference materials. A [journeyman electrician practice test](/journeyman-electrician-practice-test-guide/) built for PSI's Michigan exam (80 questions, 150 minutes, 75% to pass) looks different from PSI's Texas exam (split into a 59-question and a 26-question portion, 70% to pass each), even though both run through the exact same testing company.

That distinction matters more than people expect going in. If you find practice materials described as "the PSI journeyman exam" without naming a state, treat the specific numbers as generic, not authoritative for your test.

## What PSI actually controls versus what your state controls

PSI controls the testing experience: computer-based delivery, ID verification, scheduling, test-center security procedures, and how your score gets reported. Your state board controls the content: which NEC edition the exam references, how many questions you'll see, how much time you get, and what score counts as passing. When you register with PSI, you're registering for your state's specific exam, delivered on PSI's platform, not a generic "PSI electrician exam."

## How registration and scheduling work

Once your state board approves your application, PSI notifies you (email or mail) that you're eligible to schedule. You then register online, by phone, or by mail, pay the exam fee directly to PSI, and pick a test center and date. Most PSI programs let you reschedule without forfeiting your fee if you cancel at least 2 business days before your appointment; missing that window or arriving late typically forfeits the fee entirely ([sample PSI Candidate Information Bulletin structure, Michigan Bureau of Construction Codes program](https://proctor2.psionline.com/media/programs/755%20MI%20Electrical.pdf)).

## What to expect at the test center

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Arrival | Arrive 30 minutes early for sign-in and ID verification |
| ID check | One valid, unexpired, government-issued photo ID matching your registration name exactly |
| Personal items | Phones, smartwatches, bags, notes, and outerwear go into secure storage, not the testing room |
| Tutorial | Up to 15 minutes on-screen tutorial before the timed exam starts; this does not count against your exam time |
| Testing | One question at a time, computer-delivered, with a visible countdown timer |
| Scoring | Pass/fail result shown on screen immediately after you finish, followed by an official printed or emailed report |

## Reference materials and open-book rules

Most PSI-delivered electrical exams are open book against the NEC, but "open book" comes with specific restrictions that trip people up. Common rules across PSI programs: only soft-bound code books with factory-installed tabs are allowed (spiral-bound, loose-leaf, and ring-bound copies are typically rejected at check-in), you generally cannot write in your code book during the exam itself even if you could before it, and the NEC Handbook (the annotated commentary edition) is usually not allowed even though the plain code book is. Confirm your state's exact rules in its current PSI candidate bulletin, since these restrictions are set state by state, not by PSI uniformly.

## Calculators and scratch materials

Expect to be limited to a basic four-function, non-programmable, silent calculator without alphabet keys or paper-tape printing. Some programs provide scratch paper and a pencil at the test station instead of letting you bring your own, and collect both at the end. Bringing a graphing or programmable calculator is a common, avoidable mistake that can cost you a testing slot.

## Why "PSI journeyman electrician exam" pass rates aren't a single number

Because PSI delivers dozens of different state-specific exams under contract, there's no single national "PSI journeyman pass rate" to quote, and treating one state's published pass rate as representative of another state's exam is a mistake. If your state board publishes pass-rate data (some do, some don't), that's the only number relevant to your specific exam.

## Preparing for a PSI-delivered exam specifically

Since the content and format come from your state, not from PSI, your prep should start with your state's current candidate information bulletin, not a generic "PSI exam" guide. Once you know your actual question count, time limit, and passing score, timed practice becomes far more useful. ## FAQ

**Does PSI write the [journeyman electrician exam questions](/journeyman-electrician-exam-questions-explained/)?**
PSI typically develops and delivers the exam under contract with your state licensing board, using content outlines and requirements the board sets. The board retains authority over what's tested and what passing looks like, even though PSI builds and administers the actual exam.

**Is the PSI journeyman electrician exam the same everywhere?**
No. Question count, time limit, and passing score are set by each state board individually. Two states using PSI as their vendor can have exams that look nothing alike in length or structure.

**Can I use my phone as a calculator at a PSI test center?**
No. Phones, smartwatches, and virtually all personal electronic devices go into secure storage before you enter the testing room. Bring a standalone basic calculator if your state's exam allows one.

**What happens if I arrive late to a PSI test center?**
In most PSI programs, arriving after your scheduled start time means you are not admitted and you forfeit your examination fee. Plan to arrive at least 30 minutes early to account for parking, sign-in, and ID verification.

**How soon do I get my score after a PSI exam?**
Immediately in most cases. Your pass/fail result appears on screen right after you finish, typically followed by a printed report at the test center or an emailed score report, along with a diagnostic breakdown by content area if you didn't pass.
